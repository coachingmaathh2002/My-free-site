Content Overview

  • WHAT: Curvature as a differential geometric measure and improper integrals as extended Riemann integration
  • ABOUT: Geometric properties of curves, radius of curvature, and convergence tests for infinite domains
  • WHY: Essential applications in engineering, physics, and advanced mathematical modeling

Historical & Theoretical Foundation

Historical Reference: Curvature formalized by Leibniz & Newton; improper integrals developed by Cauchy & Riemann

Curvature Definition: κ = |dy/dx| / (1 + (dy/dx)²)^(3/2) — measures the rate of change of tangent angle

Improper Integral: ∫[a,∞) f(x)dx = lim(t→∞) ∫[a,t] f(x)dx — integral over infinite domain or unbounded integrand

Properties & Key Concepts

Curvature Properties

  • Measures bending intensity
  • Straight line: κ = 0
  • Circle: κ = 1/r (constant)

Improper Integral Types

  • Type I: Infinite limits
  • Type II: Infinite discontinuity
  • Type III: Both conditions

Application: Curvature of a Parabola

Problem: Find the curvature at vertex of y = x²

Solution:

\[\frac{dy}{dx} = 2x, \quad \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 2\]
\[\kappa = \frac{|2|}{(1 + 0)^{3/2}} = 2\]

Conclusion: At vertex (0,0), radius of curvature R = 1/κ = 0.5

Improper Integral: Convergence Example

Example: Evaluate ∫[1,∞) 1/x² dx

\[= \lim_{t \to \infty} \int_1^t x^{-2} dx = \lim_{t \to \infty} \left[-\frac{1}{x}\right]_1^t\]
\[= \lim_{t \to \infty} \left(-\frac{1}{t} + 1\right) = 1\]

Result: Integral converges to 1 (p-test: p=2 > 1)
